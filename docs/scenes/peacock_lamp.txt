Lamp evidence and choices
  CMA: https://www.clevelandart.org/art/2018.281
  Current record: c. 1902, Clara Wolcott Driscoll / Tiffany Studios. 65cm overall,
  48cm diameter. Original kerosene reservoir/double-wick/chimney AND electric
  armature survive. This is an electric configuration, not a claim of a later
  conversion. The scan shows three bulbs, a dismantled oil assembly lying beside
  the base, a display ground and nearly coincident Shade/Inner/Shell surfaces.
  The selected glTF removes the ground, the separate oil apparatus and duplicate
  shade surfaces from active scene traversal; its accessors/buffers stay unchanged.
  Parts selected by the scene: Lamp_Material (copper conductor with UV patina),
  Shade, Bulb_Glass (crown dielectric), Filament_Material (dark metal ferrules).
  The ferrule parts are chunky supports, not fine hot filaments. A geometric
  3mm-radius, .08mm-wire carbon loop inside the first bulb is the one real emitter.
  Two sockets are unlit, an explicit scene choice. No emissive shade/shell/bulb,
  no supplementary lights, no glTF emission factors adopted from the viewer.
  fit_height=65cm measures the same selected whole model for every part; the
  resulting shade diameter is about 47.5cm, consistent with the museum's 48cm.

  Carbon temperature: 2100K, based on NBS Museum working-standard description:
  https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/IR/nbsir76-1125.pdf
  Rating: choose the 4 mean-horizontal candlepower carbon bulb listed in Table 3:
  https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-C13-4ed3e0af3f8fbdbed1c8f03c529b9581/pdf/GOVPUB-C13-4ed3e0af3f8fbdbed1c8f03c529b9581.pdf
  That specification gives mean spherical / horizontal intensity = .825.
  Approximate historical candles as cd: flux = 4*.825*4*pi = 41.469 lm.
  Therefore 16cp is ~165.88lm, not ~50lm; ~50 is its wattage (16*3.1 W).
  These sources establish period possibilities, not this object's actual bulb rating.
  Loop radiance Y = flux/(pi*area*14713) = 94.689477 scene units.
  Area is 4*pi^2*.003*.00008 = 9.47482e-6 m2 (analytic torus; the polygon mesh
  approximates this area, giving a small discretisation error in luminous flux).

Stained-glass syntax: thin-slab material contract
  The material syntax is:
    type = stained_glass
    transmittance = image("...", mapping = uv)
    gain = ... density = ... thickness = 3mm index = constant(1.52) haze = 0
    glass_mask = image("...", mapping = uv) mask_threshold = .5
    stone_material = ...
  This lamp now uses the SAME type, transmittance, gain, density, thickness, index
  and haze spellings. The lamp adds reflectance, reflection_scale and roughness;
  haze=.90 specifies the opalescent limit. Masks/stone delegation are unnecessary
  because Shade is already a separate part, with lead encoded as dark texels.
  Earlier draft called these transmission_scale and diffuse_transmission. Final
  scene adopts gain and haze to avoid a competing syntax. Clear-glass defaults are
  haze=0, no diffuse reflection, roughness=0; straight transmission is preserved.

  Normative lamp proposal (wavelength by wavelength; reciprocal, two-sided slab):
  * transmittance supplies normal INTERNAL tau, sRGB decoded to linear reflectance
    spectra, not emitted radiance. tau0=clamp(gain*image_spectrum,0,1)^density.
    Defaults gain=1, density=1, thickness=3mm. density scales optical depth.
    tau0 is specified THROUGH thickness, so thickness is descriptive/reference
    thickness, not a second absorption multiplier. Other thicknesses require new
    tau0/density. This is an ideal thin slab without positional ray displacement.
  * index is the glass refractive index. At incident angle theta, Snell gives
    theta_t; tau=tau0^(1/abs(cos(theta_t))). Fresnel reflection at BOTH faces,
    with incoherent infinite inter-reflections. For symmetric air surroundings:
      Ts=(1-F)^2*tau/(1-F^2*tau^2)
      Rs=F + (1-F)^2*F*tau^2/(1-F^2*tau^2)
    Use continuous limiting values at grazing incidence. No medium stack entry.
  * haze=h in [0,1] sends (1-h)*Ts straight through, and h*Ts into a cosine-weighted
    hemisphere on the opposite face: normalized Lambertian diffuse transmission.
    h is constant, symmetric front/back and reciprocal; h=0 clear cathedral glass,
    h=1 fully opalescent transmission. It redistributes power, never generates it.
    This is a surface transport closure, not a scattering world/volume medium;
    VCM must classify the diffuse branch as connectable and mergeable.
  * reflectance supplies the diffuse reflected COLOUR, rho=reflection_scale*image
    spectrum. Rdiff=(1-Rs-Ts)*clamp(rho,0,1); remaining budget is absorbed. This
    allocation guarantees Rs+Ts+Rdiff<=1 at every wavelength/angle. Both faces
    use the same rho unless a future back_reflectance override is explicitly given.
    With rho=0 there is only Fresnel reflection, preserving the clear-glass model.
  * roughness broadens the specular reflection lobe using a reciprocal normalized
    GGX closure; it must not silently convert the straight branch to a refracted
    thick-solid ray. roughness=0 is the specular limit. Neither haze nor roughness
    changes total branch power. All branches participate in MIS and photon transport.
  * Black lead texels give tau~0 and rho~0, absorbing rather than emitting. This
    scan's lead is photographic, so residual highlights are a known approximation.
  * Mask semantics, where needed by a window mesh, delegate masked-out stone to its
    opaque material; do not interpret stone as low-transmission glass.

Lit versus unlit appearance / texture audit
  Shade_baseColor.png, Shade_emissive.jpeg and Shell_baseColor.png largely repeat
  the SAME photographed atlas; one island is markedly brighter than the other.
  Inner_baseColor.jpeg is another photograph with a different UV layout. It is
  not a registered unlit reflection measurement for the Shade mesh.
  Shade_transmission.png is a packed glTF scalar transmission-channel mask with
  cyan RGB, not a measured RGB transmission spectrum. The emissive map is a
  viewer presentation aid, not the source of the lamp's real radiance.
  Accordingly the scene uses ONE Shade colour atlas with independent strengths
  gain=.55 and reflection_scale=.18, plus haze=.90. An unlit view gets coloured
  diffuse reflection and Fresnel reflection; an internally lit view also receives
  diffuse coloured transmission. Light-state changes do not switch images.
  This is an explicitly uncalibrated material estimate. No evidence justifies two
  independent measured textures, or treating the bright atlas as measured tau.
  Baked highlights/unequal capture brightness cannot be recovered from this scan.
  Better measurements would replace the two material inputs without changing syntax.

Photometry: estimates, not measurements
  Same scale as the Observatory: 14713 photometric SI units per scene Y, anchored
  to its solar normal irradiance (6.7967 scene units for 100000 lux).
  Qwantani HDR upper-hemisphere solid-angle-weighted RGB Y=.890121381.
  HDR scale 7.635711866e-9 -> target upper-sky luminance 0.0001 cd/m2.
  This normalizes an uncalibrated photograph to a chosen moonless-night luminance,
  not a claim that Poly Haven delivered a photometrically calibrated HDR file.
  The engine's spectral RGB reconstruction makes the RGB normalization approximate.
  Sky background through crown window glass ~0.000092 cd/m2, direction-dependent;
  reflection of the room in the glass is additional, physically legitimate radiance.
  At a .55m lamp-to-wall distance, bare isotropic equivalent intensity is 3.3cd.
  With wall rho=.52, unfiltered upper-bound luminance is rho*3.3/(pi*.55^2)=1.81cd/m2.
  If local directional throughput of the shade is .05–.30, direct-wall luminance is
  .09–.54cd/m2; shadows lower, interreflection adds. Around 1m, .027–.164cd/m2.
  Thus nearby wall highlights are of order tenths to one cd/m2; a uniform 1cd/m2
  room is not justified by this dim lamp and dark wood. Actual throughput is unknown.
  Ceiling is farther away and dimmer. Haze changes the angular distribution, so
  these inverse-square figures are only a sanity envelope, not a transport result.

  Observer    exposure(log2 gain)    white balance     design purpose
  Room        +11.4                 3000K            room and glowing shade
  Shade       +6.5                  3000K            preserve transmitted feather colour
  Wall        +12.4                 3000K            soft wall/ceiling accent
  Window      +25.0                 none             sky-adapted optional eye
  At Room gain a 1cd/m2 wall maps to ~.184 pre-tonemap Y; 0.0001 sky maps to
  ~1.84e-5 (ratio 10000, 13.29 stops). It should appear black. At Window gain the
  sky maps to ~.228, while the same 1cd/m2 wall would map to ~2281 and saturate.
  Shade luminance and the suitability of +6.5 must be measured once transport exists.
  The scratch layout turns the carbon loop off and uses gain +25 for all eyes,
  viewing the diffuse stand-in in the existing physical sky light. It is a
  sky-adapted scale/layout check, not the intended illuminated-room exposure.
  The lit scratch has an opaque diffuse shade: it cannot show glass glow or wall
  colour. Do not judge the missing optical effect or claim its noise from it.

Previews, validation, timings and hardware
  python previews/peacock_lamp.py --prepare-only
  python previews/peacock_lamp.py --mode layout --views Room Shade Wall
  python previews/peacock_lamp.py --mode lit --views Room --compare
  Every render invokes build/owe render ... --backend gpu --resolution 320x200
  --spp 16. A 115s process limit keeps individual trials under two minutes.
  --compare warms both pipelines, then compares two independent VCM estimates
  against averages of path batches at approximately equal wall-clock budgets
  (overshoot <= one path batch). It saves timings and pair-difference XYZ noise
  RMS; loading/output count toward time, compilation warmup does not. This does
  not measure estimator bias. Stand-in transport is not stained-glass transport.
